## Research briefing

**1. What did we learn?**

**DL service capacity.** In the tested single-UE cell (50 RB, CQI 15, 1 ms TTI, HARQ16), DL service capacity is one ≈ 7.25 kB MAC PDU per TTI: about **55.0 Mb/s of RLC SDUs with the MaxCI scheduler (Q-0) and 54.5 Mb/s with the QoS-aware scheduler (Q-d)**. That is ≈ 1.21 × L_tot. Factor 1 (45.4 Mb/s) is served completely. At factors 2, 4 and 8 the achieved throughput stays at the plateau. The load DRB backlog then grows without bound (to 451 MB at factor 8), because the NR RLC UM TX buffer is unbounded.

**Nothing is lost.** No frame and no load SDU is lost or unexplained in any run:
- no HARQ deletion and no NACK;
- at most 6 busy HARQ processes;
- no MAC or RLC overflow.

Degradation appears **only as delay and right-censoring**. Every undelivered gPTP frame has packet-specific evidence: its own SDU is queued in the RLC TX buffer, or its delivery is still in flight at T_end.

**Overload is an option-dependent risk for gPTP.**

| Option | Behaviour from factor 2 |
|---|---|
| **Q-0** (gPTP on DRB 1, shared with the load) | Each Sync waits behind the whole backlog. Censored cohort pairs: 26 %, 62.5 % and 80 % at factors 2, 4 and 8. The longest delivered residence reaches 9.5 s. Slave updates thin out to one every 208, 415 and 827 ms |
| **Q-d (UM)** (gPTP on DRB 4) | Unaffected at every factor: P(used) = 1, Sync residence p99 4.99 ms. Its own DRB backlog stays 0, while DRB 1 grows exactly as under Q-0 |

**Timing accuracy is resilient even where delivery fails.** The sync error from the exact oracle stays ≤ 5 ns in every run, including Q-0 at factor 8. The residence correction stays exact for multi-second residences. The slave's rate-compensated holdover over gaps of up to 0.83 s adds only nanoseconds. The failure mode under overload is **update starvation (availability)**, not accuracy loss.

**Two predictions were refuted.**
- **Q-0 residence at factor 1.** Q-0 already crosses the exploratory 5.5 ms residence threshold at factor 1: one Sync of 80 at 5.95 ms. Below the 11 ms threshold, nothing crosses at factor 1.
- **Q-d uplink.** In the Q-d configuration the UE's **uplink load is served at only 2.48 of 44.8 Mb/s at every factor, including factor 1.** The UE gets a UL grant in 435 TTIs instead of 15,635 under Q-0. This is an unexplained mechanism in the Q-d / QoS-aware configuration (§6).

**2. Why does it matter for timing, reliability or resilience?**

- **Reliability.** Under the unbounded NR RLC UM buffer of Simu5G 1.8.0, overload does not cause loss. It causes unbounded delay. Any reliability metric for this regime must therefore count censored frames, and the +∞ conservative ranking matters. A loss counter alone would report "0 % loss" for a Q-0 configuration that delivers only 20 % of its Syncs.
- **Timing.** Accuracy and availability decouple. The exact residence correction keeps |e| at nanoseconds while update gaps grow to 0.8 s. A timing-SLA analysis needs an availability or holdover term, not only an error bound.
- **Resilience.** Separating gPTP onto its own prioritised DRB (Q-d) keeps time sync intact at 6.7 × the measured DL capacity. The same configuration starves the UE's uplink data, which is a cross-direction side effect that has to be explained before E1/E2 load regimes are interpreted.

**3. What remains unproven?**

- **Generality of the capacity value.** It holds for N = 1 and this radio configuration only. It is **not** a capacity threshold for N = 2, 4 or 8 (scheduler, HARQ and RB sharing differ). The factor labels 2× / 4× / 8× are not overload claims beyond what §3 shows.
- **The Q-d uplink starvation mechanism.** It has not been localised. It may lie in the QoS-aware UL scheduling path, the BSR / backlog mirror, the configuration, or an interaction specific to N = 1. No claim is made about its cause.
- **The positive control.** Whether Q8's "lost with an observed discard" attribution works on real evidence is shown here only on a synthetic mutation, and that control failed for a reason in its own construction (§7). E0 itself contains no real loss, so Q8's loss path has no real positive instance in E0.
- **Replay equivalence on the overload runs.** Equivalence of the memory-light replay with the original replay was checked only on the five runs with small queues. On the three Q-0 overload runs only Q8's own model validation applies: all 56.7–57.1 k observed deliveries were predicted, and none was unpredicted.
- **Not observed in E0.** HARQ5 behaviour (E0 used HARQ16, an experimental control, not a deployment recommendation), multi-UE interference, onset ordering across N, and any statistical spread (seed 0 only).

## 10. Implications for E1 / E2 load regimes (proposal; nothing run)

- **Capacity reference.** At N = 1, ≈ 1.21 × L_tot DL is the service capacity; 1 × L_tot is about 83 % of it. For **sym** at N > 1 the total load is unchanged, so 1 × L_tot is below the N = 1 capacity. But per-UE CQI, scheduler sharing and HARQ change with N, so E1 must **measure** saturation per N with the same evidence (growth + achieved < offered). It must not import 1.21 ×.
- **E2 factors.** Factors 2 and 4 are deep overload at N = 1 (1.65 × and 3.3 × capacity). Under Q-0 they produce censoring, not loss, and saturation is established by factor 2. An E2 sweep around **0.8–1.5 × L_tot** would resolve the onset. 4 × adds only backlog, which matches the declared escalation-stop rationale. This needs your decision.
- **Horizon.** Under overload, Q-0 reliability is horizon-dependent: the censored fraction grows with run time. Results must be reported as rates or "delivered by horizon H", never as loss.
- **Q-d uplink.** The UL starvation must be localised (read-only) before any E1 interference or UL-load conclusion is drawn for Q-d.
- **HARQ5 pairing.** E0 says nothing about HARQ5. The deletion mechanism (P1) is Simu5G-specific to the tested configuration.
