# M2-S6: non-ideal clocks across the 5G–TSN bridge (results)

**Status:** implemented and checked according to `migration/m2-s6-design.md` revision 2 (approved 2026-10-19).

**Base and stack:**
- Branch `m2/architecture` from `2ea9a03` (M2-S5); master untouched.
- OMNeT++ 6.4.0, INET 4.7.0 and Simu5G 1.8.0 unmodified; clang 18.1.3.

**Record:** `M2-S6/check.txt` = **1863/1863** at `fe8997b`. Rows: K 445, S 80, R 313, E 642, T 364, N 19. Gates D0 8/8 and E0b 3/3.

**Claim** (design decision 12): within the simulation, the INET slave clock error under constant and time-varying oscillator errors and modelled translator clock errors equals the independent oracle's prediction exactly. The error is attributed to link-delay, rate-ratio, translator-clock, downstream-Pdelay, quantization and servo components.

**Not claimed:**
- IEEE 802.1AS, IEEE 1588 or 3GPP conformance;
- standards-conformant servo or holdover behaviour;
- realistic 5G time-distribution accuracy.

**Ranking.**
- With ideal translators and 1 ns GM/slave ticks, steady error is quantization-limited (≤ 4 ns), whatever the GM or slave drift, 5G load or HARQ.
- What exceeds that:
  1. any TT time error, which passes through 1:1;
  2. TT timestamp noise (×√2 per Follow_Up, ×√5 between steps);
  3. drift ramps (D·T²);
  4. the one-interval calibration excursion of about y_G·Res, up to 546 ns.
- 5G congestion acts only through the Follow_Up lag, which multiplies whatever rate error the previous interval left (finding 6).

**R-INET cross-check** (`M2S6U-R-inet`, two INET gPTP bridges with ideal clocks, same clocks and traffic as `M2S6U-C-a`):
- The GM K row is exact.
- Slave error at the 1 ms samples from 2 s: 0…+3 ns (mean +1.60 ns), against 0…+4 ns (mean +1.68 ns) for the M2-S6 translators.
- The INET slave's compensation converges to +200.0125…+200.0273 ppm (true ratio +200.02 ppm).

## 8. Findings

1. **INET's StepClockServo resets the oscillator tick phase at every step** (`resetOscillator = true`, K7). With L > 1 ps this happens at every step: 80 of 80 steps, elapsed phase 795–9 546 ps at 10 ns. It delays both the settable and the raw clock. The predicted rate bias (H8, +L/2 per interval) is **refuted** in 29 of 29 configs: the mean between-step drift is −0.2…+0.05 ns at 1 ns ticks and −0.14 ns at 10 ns.

   Evidence:
   - **Exactness.** D0d and the K/S rows show that O1/O5 reproduce every slave clock, raw-clock and gmRR value with the resets modelled, and that the raw-clock replay fails without them. The refutation is therefore a property of the exact INET arithmetic, not of an approximation.
   - **Counterfactual** (`diagnostics/h8-Q10.txt`): the INET slave (O5) replayed on the same recorded inputs of M2S6U-Q10, with and without the K7 resets.
     - With resets: mean drift −0.143 ns, mean gmRR − 1 = +200 067.8 ppb.
     - Without resets: −0.286 ns, +200 019.7 ppb.
     - The resets raise gmRR by 48.1 ppb. The mean reset phase alone (7.14 ns per 125 ms) would give 57.1 ppb.
     - The resets shorten the raw time base the rate is measured against, and the servo's absolute rate compensation absorbs that. No net drift remains with or without resets.
   - The counterfactual is a diagnostic only: without resets the slave's own Pdelay timers would fire at slightly different times. It is not a checker row.
2. **The step rounds the new clock value up to the tick** (K8). With 1 ns ticks a step reveals the measured offset only to one tick. The 1 ps closure configs give old − new = o_k exactly.
3. **A 1 ps drifting tick works** (rev. 1 said otherwise). A 0 ppm, 1 ps ConstantDriftOscillator is byte-identical to IdealOscillator (D0c).
4. **The uncalibrated first second costs about y_G·Res:** −405 ns at +100 ppm in the 5GS. The first calibrated step then leaves a one-interval excursion of the same size (H6b, about 410 ns), as the gmRR leakage identity predicts.
5. **Steady state with exact rate arithmetic is quantization-limited.** With 1 ns GM/slave ticks and ideal translators, ε is within about ±1 ns and the between-step error within 1–4 ns, idle or under load, even with Follow_Up lags of up to 125 ms. The lag term stays small because the slave's rate has converged. The exception is the one step after calibration (finding 6).
6. **The lag term is the previous interval's rate leakage times the Follow_Up lag.** StepClockServo applies the gmRR of the interval that ended at Follow_Up k−1. That gmRR contains the change of ε over that interval, (ε_k−1 − ε_k−2)/T. The clock then runs with this error during the lag between Sync reception and Follow_Up processing: λ_k ≈ (ε_k−1 − ε_k−2)/(s2_k−1 − s2_k−2) × lag_k.
   - **5GS B-m100:** at the first steady step (Follow_Up 8, lag 19.998 ms) the prediction is −78.013 ns and λ = −78 ns. The cause is the ε drop of 491.5 ns at calibration (Follow_Up 7). All later λ are 0.
   - **E-all-load (lag 125 ms):** λ −62…+70 ns, predicted to within 1 ns at every step.
   - Diagnostic: `results/M2-S6/diagnostics/lag-*.txt`. Its values come from the same O3/O5 decomposition as the check rows, rerun on regenerated runs.
   - This is why H1c fails in E-all-load: its bound has no lag term.
7. **The 5G time-reference disagreement passes through unchanged:** E_res,TT = r·Δθ exactly, and the steady e⁺ equals Δθ (+0.8 ns quantization). The common part cancels.
8. **Free-running TT clocks** (±100 ppb, no 5G time updates) let the TTs drift apart by 2 × 100 ppb × t: ε reaches −2 µs at 10 s. The rate-ratio conversion residual (y_DS − y_NW)·Res is only 28 ps over 141 µs. The time error dominates, not the rate conversion.
9. **TT noise is amplified as predicted:** std(ε) = √2·σ and std(e⁻) = √5·std(ε), within the 95 % CIs at σ = 1, 10 and 100 ns and in the combined 5GS runs.
10. **Unsmoothed NW-TT link delay under noise:** with σ = 100 ns over a 50 ns link, the single-exchange link delay is often negative. The NW-TT then reports "not measuring", and 16–32 of 80 Follow_Ups per run are INVALID (noLinkDelay) and discarded fail-closed. Steps are missed, and the between-step maximum grows to 2.4 µs.
11. **The drifting GM shifts Sync timing against the TTI grid:** in the 5GS runs with the GM at +100 ppm (B-p100, C-a and the D configs) Res is 4.0–4.5 ms, against 5–6 ms in M2-S5, and the idle Follow_Up lag is 1.7 µs instead of 4–20 ms. At −100 ppm (B-m100) Res is 4.5–6.0 ms and the lag stays at 4–20 ms; at +10 ppm (B-p10) the lag ranges from 1.7 µs to 16 ms. This is not an error, but the 5GS results with drift depend on that phase, through λ (finding 6).
12. **The downstream link delay is in DS-TT time** but applied as GM time: E_ldn,base = −y_G·Ldn = ∓5 ps at ±100 ppm, as predicted (§4.7 iv).

## 9. Limitations

- The 5G reference is ideal (simulation time). The 5G time error is only the synthetic θ0/θosc of the A2 model; the magnitudes are not 3GPP budgets [unverified].
- The TT timers run on simulation time. The GM and slave have no phase noise, only tick quantization.
- The servo is INET's StepClockServo. Results do not generalise to PI servos or to an 802.1AS servo [unverified].
- Random drift is a frequency random walk only; there is no profile oscillator (deferred, decision 6).
- R-INET is a cross-check on the slave error only.
- The H8 counterfactual and the lag diagnostic (§8) are diagnostics on regenerated runs, not checker rows.
- Per-stage records (`stage-A…F.txt`) were made at each stage's commit. The final `check.txt` is the cumulative check at `fe8997b` over all stages.
- Unchanged from M2-S5: DL only, one UE, domain 0, static roles, simulation-only Suffix, wire cf truncated to whole ns, no Announce/BMCA, no syncReceiptTimeout.
- Not claimed: IEEE 802.1AS, IEEE 1588 or 3GPP compliance; standards-conformant servo or holdover; realistic 5G time-distribution accuracy. The semantics still needing normative verification are listed in design rev. 2 §9.
