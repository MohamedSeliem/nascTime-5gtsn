- **Seeds barely vary the QOS_PF runs.** Q-0p and Q-d (UM) produce byte-identical outputs across all seeds, so their intervals do not come from independent replications (§9).
- **Three hypotheses were refuted** (§5): the DS-TT hold does not cover a Follow_Up that overtakes its Sync (split DRBs); RLC AM reorders Sync and Follow_Up under load; and my own earlier finding of 4–9 ms "MAC queueing" on DRB 1, which was a matcher artefact.

### 4.3 Selection (design §7)

- **Eligibility:** every option (gates per option, C1, Q8, Q1, H0, Q4, Q7).
- **Idle guard** (≤ Q-0 + 1 ms on p99 lag and residence): Q-a, Q-b, Q-d (UM), Q-d (AM) pass; Q-c and Q-c′ fail (+∞).
- **Phase 1 ranking:** Q-a, Q-d (UM), Q-d (AM), Q-b. Q-a and Q-d (UM) tie on load p99 lag (+∞), idle p99 lag (0.912 µs) and load p99 residence (+∞). Q-a then leads on the simplicity keys (MAXCI before QOS_PF). Experimental: Q-d′.
- **Phase 2** (sustained load): Q-0, Q-a, Q-d′.
- **Outcome:** no candidate is strictly better than Q-0 under every load condition, because every option's 5-HARQ load p99 is +∞. **Recommended: Q-0, by the rule as written. This is a conclusion among the tested candidates only, not a claim of optimality.**
- **Next-ranked candidate:** under the user's clarification, Q-0 is retained rather than testing Q-d (UM) under sustained load. Q-d (UM) already has p99 = +∞ under 0–3 s load at 5 HARQ (P = 0.9494), so under this rule it cannot rank above Q-0 either.

**The rule cannot separate the options at 5 HARQ.** The +∞ rule makes the 5-HARQ load conditions decisive, and there MAC discards affect every DRB. The 16-HARQ rows are where the DRB mechanisms differ; there Q-a and Q-d (UM) remove the lag, and Q-d (UM) also keeps residence at 5.0 ms. Changing the criterion is outside S7.1's authority; it is listed as an open decision (§9).

## 5. Hypotheses and predictions

### 5.1 Refuted or failed

1. **"A Follow_Up may overtake its Sync on split DRBs; the DS-TT hold handles it."** Refuted.
   - On Q-c, 1 628 Follow_Ups (Q-c′: 1 708; Q-d′: 24) reached the DS-TT before their Sync did. The hold covers only a Sync already queued at the DS-TT; no such case occurred in any run (0 holds, replay 0).
   - Each overtaking Follow_Up was discarded fail-closed (`fuNoSync`); none reached the slave before its Sync.
   - Delivery fell to 0.42–0.48 (Q-c, Q-c′) and 0.991 (Q-d′, idle).
2. **"RLC AM on the gPTP DRB is no worse than UM apart from header overhead."** Refuted under load. With 5 HARQ, AM retransmissions put the Sync after its Follow_Up (Q-b: 25 late pairs; Q-d (AM): 40), and the delivered-pair lag grows to 83 ms (Q-b). Idle AM lag is 1–2 ms, against 0.912 µs for UM.
3. **"4–9 ms MAC queueing on DRB 1 under load."** This was my interim finding; it was wrong. The sequential matcher went out of step after each lost SDU. With multiset matching, every delivered SDU on every DRB is delivered exactly 4 ms after its last PDU, including Q-0 under load (§3.1 Q0b-2).

### 5.2 Confirmed

- The M1 O-tail mechanism explains the Q-0 lag exactly (Q0b-1…3): the Follow_Up's tail is cut by grant slack and waits for the next SDU on DRB 1.
- A dedicated gPTP DRB with no other SDU leaves one SDU per slot, so the Follow_Up is never cut (Q0b-5, Q0b-1 on DRB 4/5).
- QOS_PF with `nasc-gptp` priority 10 lowers Sync residence by 1.0 ms idle, and holds it at 5.0 ms under load with 16 HARQ (Q-d vs Q-a).
- N-prio, priorities inverted: delivery 0.9241 vs 0.9494 — worse.

## 7. Deviations from the design, and implementation fixes

| # | Deviation / fix | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D-1 | P0(ii) compares Q-0 accuracy runs with M2-S6 below 5 s, not 10 s | The M2-S5/S6 5GS configs run 5 s |
| D-2 | Q-d implemented as two options, Q-d (UM) and Q-d (AM) | The design's Q-d leaves the RLC mode open |
| D-3 | Q3 bound variants at fixed B = 5, 6, 10 ms | Percentile bounds would depend on the run being checked |
| D-4 | M0 (d): PCAP identical and fingerprint differs | Logs that record packet tree ids shift when one extra message exists |
| D-5 | P0(ii): a constant `treeId` offset per run is attributed, every other column must match | OMNeT++ message ids come from a process-wide counter, not reset between the seeds of one process; the offset is k × 87 849 (the messages a 12 s run creates beyond a 5 s run) |
| D-6 | N-horizonShort evaluated over every seed-0 run with a 20 s twin | The design names no config; on Q-0 idle every cohort pair resolves by 9.89 s, so 10.001 s cannot cut one |
| D-7 | N-swap: "other rows unaffected" = their verdicts equal Q-c's | Q-c itself has late pairs, so "all used" was the wrong expectation |
| D-8 | Q5 hold replay = M2-S5 R-2 (Follow_Up arriving while its Sync is queued); earlier Follow_Ups reported with their discard | The interim replay counted them as hold cases |
| D-9 | Q0b-1 passes on "cut iff grant < need"; grant < demand is reported | Under load the scheduler grants below demand for lack of resources |
| D-10 | H0 runs (seed 0) before the phase-1 selection | H0 is an eligibility row |
| D-11 | `all` runs as stages `all1`…`all4` | So that each stage fits a bounded session; same steps and order |
| F-1 | Replay matching: multiset (time, size) comparison; PDU→RX PDU matching per size within 0–4 ms, not order-preserving | HARQ reorders transport blocks; a sequential matcher cascades after a loss |
| F-2 | At a horizon, an unreceived PDU is in flight unless its 4 ms reception window has closed | Found by N-horizonShort: a pair was classed lost instead of censored |
| F-3 | Q0b-3: UE→DS-TT FIFO model (constant 866 000 ps, or back to back) | A single delay constant failed whenever a Follow_Up queued behind its Sync |

## 9. Scientific limitations and claims

**Claimed (simulation-level, this model and configuration set):**
- The DRB 1 Follow_Up lag in Simu5G 1.8.0 with this traffic is fully explained by RLC UM segmentation with grant slack and the next-SDU release (exact replay).
- A dedicated UM DRB for gPTP (QFI 10, DRB 4) removes it: 0.912 µs in every tested condition where the pair is delivered.
- Splitting Sync and Follow_Up across DRBs breaks their ordering, and the M2-S5 DS-TT hold does not repair it.
- With 5 HARQ processes, MAC discards dominate the tail under load for every option.

**Not claimed:**
- That any option is optimal, or better than options not tested. Only Q-0, Q-a and Q-d′ were run under sustained load.
- That Q-0 is the better choice in practice. Its recommendation follows from the rule as written, which the 5-HARQ losses make insensitive to the DRB mechanism.
- That the 1 ms idle guard is a standards requirement. It is an engineering criterion; differences are reported in µs.
- That the AM results are predicted. They are measured-only (Q0b-4 NOT EVALUATED).
- Any statistical difference between Q-0 and Q-a delivery under load (overlapping intervals; 5 seeds).
- That the seeds are independent replications for the QOS_PF options. Their runs are almost seed-invariant: Q-0p and Q-d (UM) give one distinct output (all PCAPs byte-identical) across their 5 or 10 seeds, Q-d (AM) one or two, Q-d′ two. Their ± 0.0000 intervals therefore rest on one or two distinct runs, not five or ten. MAXCI runs differ per seed (UE-side captures). The seed-dependent draws in Simu5G 1.8.0 include UE random access (`LteMacUe.cc:1005, 1059`) and the PF/QoS score jitter (`QoSAwareScheduler.cc:213`); which of them makes MAXCI runs vary was not isolated.
- IEEE 802.1AS or 3GPP compliance, real-RAN timing, multiple UEs, UL synchronisation.

**Open decisions for review:**
1. Should selection use 16-HARQ results, or delivery probability and delivered-only p99 as separate keys, instead of the +∞ p99 at 5 HARQ?
2. Should Q-d (UM) be run under sustained load (about 2.3 GB of outputs)?
3. N-noHold needs a run in which hold cases occur (a congested DS-TT egress) to demonstrate Q5's sensitivity in this topology.
