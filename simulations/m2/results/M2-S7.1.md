## 5. Hypotheses and findings

### 5.1 Refuted or failed

1. **The DS-TT hold does not cover Follow_Up-before-Sync overtaking.** On split DRBs, 1 628 (Q-c), 1 708 (Q-c′) and 24 (Q-d′) Follow_Ups reached the DS-TT before their Sync. Each was discarded fail-closed (`fuNoSync`). The hold was not redefined to cover them. It acts as designed when its precondition occurs (§5.2).
2. **RLC AM reorders Sync and Follow_Up under load.** With 5 HARQ: Q-b has 25 late pairs and Q-d (AM) 40. Q-b's used-pair lag reaches 83 ms.
3. **"4–9 ms MAC queueing on DRB 1"** (first-round interim finding) was a matcher artefact. Every delivered SDU arrives exactly 4 ms after its last PDU.
4. **First-round statement that UE random access is a likely source of seed variation:** refuted. Varying the UE MAC's RNG alone changes nothing (§5.3).
5. **First-round N-noHold control:** ineffective, because no hold case can arise in the Q-c idle scenario. Superseded by the targeted scenario (§6.2).

### 5.2 Confirmed

- The O-tail mechanism explains the Q-0 lag exactly (Q0b-1…3). A dedicated gPTP DRB is never cut (Q0b-5, Q0b-1).
- **The DS-TT hold, positive control.** When a Sync is queued below `TtSyncEgress` (egress gate closed) and its Follow_Up arrives meanwhile:
  - the Follow_Up is held and released behind the Sync;
  - its correction uses the Sync's actual transmission time (residence = PCAP, gate wait included);
  - the slave uses every pair.
- With the hold disabled, every such Follow_Up is discarded (pairs late), and Q5 detects it.
- QOS_PF with `nasc-gptp` priority holds Sync residence at 5.0 ms in every condition (Q-d (UM), Q-d′).

### 5.3 Seed invariance (`diagnostics/seeds.txt`, `diagnostics/rng-callsites-*.txt`)

**Method.**
- **Isolate groups.** Diagnostic configs `M2S71-R-*` (not comparable to the primary runs) give each module group its own physical RNG:
  - 0: direct `getEnvir()->getRNG(0)` callers;
  - 1: clocks;
  - 2: UE MAC;
  - 3: UE NIC rest;
  - 4: gNB MAC;
  - 5: gNB NIC rest;
  - 6: applications;
  - 7: everything else.
- **Count draws.** A diagnostic monitor counts the numbers drawn per RNG.
- **Vary one at a time.** Configs `-v<k>` vary only RNG k over seeds 0–4.
- **Find call sites.** A gdb probe counts every draw by call site in 2 s of Q-0 idle and Q-d (UM) idle.

**Draws per group** (Q-0 / Q-d (UM), idle, 12 s): RNG 0: 4 023 / 5 778; UE MAC: 521 / 521; UE NIC rest: 2 522 / 2 570; gNB NIC rest: 7 139 / 7 139; others: 0–3. Call sites:
- RNG 0: the BLER-table constructor (`PhyPisaData`, 4 000 draws at start-up); in QOS_PF, also the score jitter (`QoSAwareScheduler::prepareSchedule`); in MAXCI, the score tie-break (`SortedDesc::operator<`, `LteScheduler.h:43`; 3 draws in 2 s of Q-0 idle).
- UE MAC: random access (`checkRAC`).
- NIC: Jakes fading, path loss, shadowing, reception success.

**Influence** (distinct runs out of 5; cohort pairs that change outcome, lag or residence against seed 0):

| Varied RNG | Q-0 idle | Q-0 load | Q-d (UM) idle | Q-d (UM) load | Q-d (AM) idle | Q-d′ idle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (tie-break / jitter, BLER table) | 5 runs; 8–12 pairs | 5 runs; 1–5 pairs | 1 run | 1 run | 1 run | 2 runs; 1 pair |
| 2 UE MAC (random access) | 1 run | 1 run | 1 run | 1 run | — | — |
| 3 UE NIC (PHY, channel) | 1 run | 1 run | 1 run | 1 run | — | — |
| 5 gNB NIC (PHY, channel) | 1 run | 1 run | 1 run | 1 run | — | — |
| 7 other | 1 run | 1 run | 1 run | 1 run | — | — |

**Conclusions** (simulation-level, for these configurations):
- **Only RNG 0 changes anything, through the scheduler's tie handling.**
  - MAXCI breaks every exact score tie with a coin flip, which shifts individual pairs by whole slots (1 ms) and occasionally changes which pairs are lost. That is why the MAXCI options differ per seed while their p50/p99 summaries mostly coincide.
  - QOS_PF adds a ±5·10⁻⁷ jitter to scores that differ by more than that unless two connections share a profile, so the order never changes in Q-0p and Q-d (UM).
  - In Q-d′, DRBs 4 and 5 share the `nasc-gptp` profile, so the jitter does decide between them: two distinct runs. Q-d (AM) shows 2 distinct runs of 10 in the primary set; RNG 0 alone did not vary it in 5 seeds, so its source is not isolated.
- **Random access, fading, path loss, shadowing and reception draws have no influence on any output** in these single-UE, static configurations.
- **Consequence for reporting.** Deterministic groups are single observations. No interval is reported for them, and no comparison is drawn between a deterministic group and a seeded one. No randomness was added.

## 9. Scientific limitations and claims

**Claimed** (simulation-level, this model and configuration set):
- The DRB 1 Follow_Up lag is fully explained by RLC UM segmentation with grant slack and the next-SDU release (exact replay).
- A dedicated UM gPTP DRB removes it: 0.912 µs wherever the pair is used, including sustained load.
- The DS-TT hold works when its precondition occurs. Split DRBs create the opposite case (Follow_Up before Sync), which the hold does not and is not meant to cover.
- With 5 HARQ processes, MAC discards destroy PDUs on every DRB under load and dominate the conservative tail. With 16 they destroy none in these configurations.
- In these configurations the seed influences outputs only through the scheduler's tie handling.

**Not claimed:**
- Optimality of any option, or a recommendation beyond the formal rule's Q-0 among the tested candidates.
- Any operational recommendation from the exploratory 5/16-HARQ comparison.
- A reliability guarantee from 16 HARQ processes.
- Standards compliance (IEEE 802.1AS, 3GPP).
- Statistical differences between options whose intervals overlap, or between a deterministic observation and a seeded group.
- Predicted AM behaviour (measured-only).
- Generality of the seed-invariance result beyond one static UE with these channel settings.
- Real-RAN timing, multiple UEs, UL synchronisation.
