## What S2 is

`nasctime.tt.PassThroughTt` (`src/nasctime/tt/PassThroughTt.ned`) is INET's `TsnSwitch` with every TSN feature left off: a two-port 802.1Q bridge, store-and-forward, MAC learning. It holds the place of a translator and does nothing a translator does (no timestamping, no PCP/VLAN mapping, no residence-time handling). Port convention: `ethg[0]` faces the 5G system, `ethg[1]` the TSN side.

`M1Bridge` gains `hasPassThroughTts` (default `false`):
- `dsTt` between `ue[0]` and `talker`;
- `nwTt` between the UPF's N6 Ethernet port (`upf.dnEthg`) and `listener`.

Off, the network is exactly the S1 network. `[Config S2]` = S1 + `hasPassThroughTts=true`, nothing else. `[Config S2-pcap]` adds a PCAP recorder on each translator port (seed 0 only).

**Run method change (applies to S1 too):** NED path is now `<nascTime>/simulations : <nascTime>/src : <Simu5G>/src : <INET>/src` with `-x nasctime.nodes`. The legacy package `nasctime.nodes` does not load on Simu5G 1.8.0 (`NRUeDsTt.ned:26`: "Gate 'ethg' must be a vector gate"), so it is excluded; no legacy file is changed.

## Prediction miss: +1090 ns, not +2180 ns

**Initial prediction:** each direction crosses both translators, so delay should rise by two hops of 1040 ns (store-and-forward) + 50 ns (10 m cable) = 2180 ns.

**Measured:** exactly one hop, +1090 ns, constant.

**Explanation:** in this configuration the translator in front of the 5G system costs nothing, because the 5G system releases frames only on 1 ms slot boundaries and the translator's 1090 ns does not push any frame past one. The PCAPs show this (seed 0):

| Direction | Frames enter 5G system (offset in slot) | Frames leave 5G system (offset in slot) |
|---|---|---|
| UL (dsTt → UE … UPF → nwTt) | 2130 ns, every frame | 1275 ns + k × 1136 ns (k = 0, 1, 2) |
| DL (nwTt → UPF … UE → dsTt) | 2130 ns, every frame | 1090 ns, every frame |

- Without translators, frames reach the 5G system 1090 ns earlier (1040 ns into the slot). With them, they arrive at 2130 ns. Both are inside the same slot, so the frames are released at the same instant.
- The translator after the 5G system adds its full 1090 ns.
- UL frames released in the same slot leave the UPF back to back. 1136 ns = 130 B + 12 B inter-frame gap at 1 Gb/s.

The checker now predicts 1090 ns and adds the slot-alignment check that explains it. The initial 2180 ns prediction and its failure are recorded here, not hidden.

**Scope of the +1090 ns.** It is a property of this configuration, not of the translators. It holds because:
- every frame is generated on a slot boundary: 20 ms period, offsets in whole ms, numerology 0 with 1 ms slots;
- every frame arrives early enough that 1090 ns more still makes the same slot: 1 Gb/s links, 10 m cables, 130 B frames, an unloaded system.

With other phases, frame lengths, link rates or load, the front translator's delay can show up end to end, in whole slots. The addendum below tests exactly this.

**Consequence for later stages:** a translator's delay on the ingress side of the 5G system is hidden end to end unless it pushes a frame past a slot boundary. S3–S5 must measure translator timing at the translator (PCAP or signals), not only end to end.

## Addendum: phase-offset test of the explanation

**Run:** `simulations/m1/s2_phase_offset.sh`, method in `check/s2_phase.py`. Results are in `S2-phase/check.txt`: **PASS**, 10/10 rows (commit `0122872`).

**Method:** all generation times are shifted by a phase p (a command-line override of `initialProductionOffset`; Configs S1 and S2 unchanged).
1. On **S1 alone**, bisection (1 ns resolution, short runs) finds the smallest p at which frames miss their slot: p1.
2. Prediction: S2 frames arrive 1090 ns later, so S2 must miss its slot from p1 − 1090 ns. delay(S2) − delay(S1) must therefore:
   - stay at 1090 ns below p1 − 1090 and from p1 on;
   - be something else in between.
3. Full 5 s runs (seed 0) check this 1 ns either side of both edges.

| Direction | p1 (from S1 alone) | Shift at p1−1091 | p1−1089 | p1−1 | p1+1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UL | 998 911 ns | 1090 ns | 1 001 090 ns | 1 001 090 ns | 1090 ns |
| DL | 998 725 ns | 1090 ns | 1 001 090 ns | 1 001 090 ns | 1090 ns |

All 488 (UL) and 490 (DL) packets behave identically at each point.

**What it shows:**
- The prediction holds to the nanosecond. Inside the 1090 ns window, every packet is late by the hop plus exactly one 1 ms slot.
- **UL:** frames reaching the UE later than 1 000 000 ns after the slot start miss it.
- **DL:** the edge is 185 ns earlier at the UPF (999 815 ns), matching the 185 ns UPF → gNB N3 hop seen in S2's UL exit offsets. In both directions the deadline is the next slot boundary at the RAN node.
- In the S2 configuration (p = 0), frames arrive about 998 µs before that deadline, which is why the front translator is absorbed there.

## Conclusion

Pass-through translators can be inserted at the DS-TT and NW-TT positions using unmodified INET/Simu5G modules.
- Frames pass through them unchanged, with zero processing delay.
- Counts are unaffected.
- In this configuration, the end-to-end effect is an exact constant (+1090 ns), explained by slot alignment and confirmed by the phase-offset test.

S3 adds VLAN/PCP/QFI handling on this topology.
