## What S4 is

`[Config S4]` is the S2 network (pass-through translators) with gPTP between the two stations:
- **Roles:** `listener` (behind nwTt) is the gPTP grandmaster; `talker` (behind dsTt) is the gPTP slave.
- **Station type:** both become INET `TsnDevice` (StandardHost + `Gptp` + `SettableClock`).
  - `M1Bridge` now declares them `<default("StandardHost")> like INetworkNode`.
  - Only a config that sets their typename changes them; S1–S3 are unchanged (regression below).
- **Clocks:** ideal (`IdealOscillator`) at both stations, so every slave clock error comes from the path.
- **gPTP settings:** INET defaults. Two-step Sync every 125 ms; Pdelay every 1 s; pdelay smoothing 0.1; `StepClockServo`.
- **Not time-aware:** translators and 5G system. Nothing adds residence time or a correction field, as planned for M1.
- **Other traffic:** the S2 talker/listener flows keep running.
- **One required setting:** INET's gPTP timestamps at the *start* of transmission and reception. INET's default store-and-forward PHY receiver (`PacketReceiver`) does not signal reception start, and gPTP stops with an error. The two station links therefore use INET's `StreamingTransmitter` / `DestreamingReceiver`, as INET's own gPTP showcase does: both station PHYs plus the translator port facing each station. This is ini only.

## Clock behaviour (model consistency, no accuracy claim)

These checks do not ask whether the slave is accurate; it is not, by design of M1. They ask whether the slave clock does exactly what IEEE 802.1AS (as implemented in INET) predicts from the path delays measured independently in the PCAPs.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| GM timestamps: Follow_Up `preciseOriginTimestamp` = start of the Sync at the GM | PASS, 39/39, 0.000 ns. This also validates the start-of-frame times taken from the PCAPs. |
| pdelay: the slave's samples = INET's estimator `(r(t4 − t1) − (t3 − t2))/2`, smoothed by 0.1, from measured times | PASS, 5/5, max error 0.063 ns |
| GM rate ratio = from successive measured Sync arrivals and origin timestamps | PASS, 38/38, max error 3e-14 |
| Every slave clock step: offset after each Follow_Up = `t1 + pdelay − ts + (tf − ts) × compensation` | PASS, 39/39, max error 0.001 ns |

**What the model shows** (seed 0):

- **pdelay is the 5G system, not a link.** Each raw measurement is half the UL+DL round trip through the 5G system: 7.0, 16.9, 10.3, 14.4 and 17.6 ms.
  - UL legs: 9.0–30.2 ms. They vary with the UL grant pattern seen since S0.
  - DL legs: 5.0 ms.
  - The smoothed estimate ends at 9.7 ms.
- **Asymmetry becomes offset.** Sync takes 5.0 ms one way (6.0 ms for 6 of 39), but the slave assumes pdelay. Each step therefore leaves the slave ahead of the grandmaster by about pdelay − Sync delay. After the first Follow_Up, 90 % of the offset samples lie between 1.3 and 4.9 ms; the peak is 6.4 ms.
- **Delay variation becomes rate error.**
  - A 1 ms change in Sync delay between two Syncs 125 ms apart reads as a −7937 to +15 127 ppm GM rate ratio.
  - The slave applies it as an oscillator compensation, even though both clocks are ideal.

**Baseline, 10 seeds** (`S4/clock-baseline.txt`, recorded only):

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---|
| Max \|offset\| | 5.3–6.4 ms |
| Mean \|offset\| after 1 s | 3.2–3.6 ms |
| Offset at 5 s | 4.4–4.8 ms |

This is the uncompensated reference that M2's time-aware translators must improve on.

## Open observation (not attributed)

**O-S4-1: Follow_Up messages take much longer through the 5G system than their Sync.**

| Message (DL) | One-way delay |
|---|---|
| Sync | 5.0 or 6.0 ms |
| Follow_Up (sent about 2 µs after its Sync, same flow and DRB) | 10, 15, 20 or 25 ms (≈ 10 each) |
| Pdelay_Resp_Follow_Up | 5–21 ms |

- The pattern is in multiples of 5 ms.
- Possible link to O-S3-2 (a segmented SDU's tail waiting for the next grant) and to the 5-slot HARQ round trip. Not analysed.
- It does not affect two-step accuracy, which uses only Sync timing, nor any S4 check.
- It matters for M2: it is a DL latency effect on back-to-back frames on one DRB.

## Conclusion

- gPTP messages cross the unmodified Simu5G 1.8.0 Ethernet bridge and the pass-through translators intact, in both directions, on the default QoS flow.
- With ideal clocks, the slave's errors are fully and exactly explained by the measured 5G-system path: delay asymmetry into offset, delay variation into rate ratio.
- Transport and clock behaviour were verified separately.
- S5 (metadata survival) is next.
